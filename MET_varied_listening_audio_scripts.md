# MET Listening Practice — Varied Audio Scripts

## Teacher Notes

These are **original practice scripts** designed in the style of the three Listening formats used in the MET sample test: **Part 1 short conversations**, **Part 2 longer conversations**, and **Part 3 short talks**. Students should hear each recording **only once**. For Parts 2 and 3, encourage students to take short notes while listening.

---

# Part 1 — Short Conversations

# Part 2 — Longer Conversations

## Conversation 1 — Choosing an Internship

**Woman:** Hi, Daniel. Do you have a minute? I need some advice about internships.

**Man:** Sure. Have you found any interesting ones?

**Woman:** Two, actually. One is at a marketing company downtown. The work sounds creative, and it pays well. The problem is that I would spend nearly two hours commuting every day.

**Man:** That is a long trip. What is the other option?

**Woman:** It is with a small environmental organization near my apartment. I would help organize community events and write short articles for its website. It is unpaid, though.

**Man:** Which one fits your long-term goals better?

**Woman:** Probably the environmental organization. I want to work in communications, but I also care about sustainability. Still, I need to save money for next semester.

**Man:** Could you work part-time at the organization and keep your weekend job?

**Woman:** I asked. They need someone three afternoons a week, so I could continue working at the bookstore on Saturdays.

**Man:** That sounds manageable. You would gain relevant experience without giving up all your income.

**Woman:** You are right. I think I was focusing too much on the salary. I will accept the position near my apartment and adjust my schedule.

## Talk 2 — Campus Podcast: Study Breaks

Welcome to this week’s campus podcast. Today, we are discussing a study habit that many students ignore: taking effective breaks.

When students feel pressure before an exam, they often believe that studying continuously is the best solution. However, concentration naturally decreases when people work on the same task for a long period. A short break can help students return to their work with more attention and energy.

The key word here is “short.” Watching several episodes of a television series or spending an hour on social media may make it difficult to start studying again. Instead, choose an activity that helps your mind rest without taking over your entire afternoon. You might walk around the block, prepare a snack, stretch, or call a friend for a few minutes.

Finally, plan your breaks before you begin. For example, after forty-five minutes of focused study, take ten minutes away from your desk. By treating breaks as part of your study plan rather than as a distraction, you may work more efficiently and feel less stressed.

## Talk 3 — Public Library Lecture

Tonight’s lecture is about the changing role of public libraries. Many people still think of libraries mainly as places to borrow books. Books remain important, of course, but modern libraries often provide many other services.

For example, some libraries offer free access to computers, printers, and reliable internet connections. This can be especially valuable for people who need to complete job applications or communicate with government services online. Libraries may also run classes in digital skills, language learning, or financial planning.

Another important role is creating a shared public space. Unlike cafés or private offices, libraries generally welcome people without expecting them to spend money. Students can study, parents can bring children to story time, and local groups can use meeting rooms for community projects.

This does not mean that every library should offer exactly the same programs. The most successful libraries pay attention to the needs of the people they serve. In one neighborhood, that may mean more children’s activities. In another, it may mean help for job seekers or support for older residents who want to learn how to use new technology.

# Recording Guide

- Use **two distinct voices** for each Part 1 and Part 2 conversation.
- Read at a **natural, clear pace**; avoid exaggerated pauses between every sentence.
- Add a **2-second pause** after Part 1 conversations before reading the question.
- Add a **4-second pause** after each Part 2 or Part 3 recording before students answer the questions.
- Do **not** repeat the recordings during test-style practice.
- For learning mode, play the recording once, let students answer, then play it again for transcript-based correction.
