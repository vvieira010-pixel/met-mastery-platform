Urban Farms in the City

Over the last decade, urban farming has moved from a niche idea to a practical solution in many cities. Vacant lots, unused rooftops, and even old parking spaces are being transformed into productive gardens. These farms supply fresh vegetables to local residents, restaurants, and food banks, often at a lower cost than imported produce. Supporters say the farms improve access to healthy food, create small business opportunities, and reduce the distance food must travel before reaching consumers.

Urban farming also offers environmental benefits. Plants help cool hot city blocks, and green spaces can absorb rainwater that might otherwise overload drainage systems. In some neighborhoods, community gardens have become places where children learn how food grows and neighbors work together on shared projects. However, the movement is not without problems. Soil contamination, limited space, and high land prices can make it difficult to start or maintain a farm. In addition, some critics question whether urban farms can produce enough food to matter on a larger scale.

Despite these concerns, interest in urban farming continues to grow. City governments, nonprofit groups, and private businesses are investing in projects that support local agriculture. For many residents, the appeal is simple: they like the idea of eating food that was grown nearby and seeing unused land turned into something useful.

Text A — Company Notice: Remote Work Policy Update

"Starting next month, all employees may work remotely up to three days per week. Employees who choose to work remotely must ensure they are reachable by phone or email during core hours from 9:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. All projects and deadlines remain unchanged regardless of work location. Employees who prefer to work in the office five days a week may continue to do so. Please speak with your supervisor if you have questions about how this policy applies to your team."

Text B — Employee Comment

"I think the new policy is a good step forward. I have been working from home on Fridays for the past year, and I am much more productive without the long commute. However, I do miss the casual conversations with colleagues that used to happen in the office. Those moments helped us solve small problems before they became big ones. I hope the company encourages teams to schedule regular check-ins so remote workers do not feel disconnected from the rest of the team."

Text C — Article: The Changing Workplace

"Remote work has become one of the most significant shifts in the modern workplace. Research indicates that many workers report higher job satisfaction when they have flexibility in where they work. However, the same studies show that remote employees can feel isolated if communication is not prioritized. Companies that implement structured virtual meetings and clear communication guidelines tend to have more successful remote work programs. The key is finding a balance that works for both the organization and its employees."

Text A — School Announcement: New Lunch Menu

"Starting this semester, our school is introducing an updated lunch menu with more fresh fruits, vegetables, and whole grains. The changes follow new nutritional guidelines designed to support student health and concentration during class. Popular items such as pizza and chicken sandwiches will still be available but will be prepared with healthier ingredients. Students are encouraged to try the new options, and feedback cards are available in the cafeteria. Parents can view the full menu on the school website."

Text B — Student Opinion

"I was not sure about the new menu at first, but after trying it for a few weeks, I actually like most of the changes. The fresh fruit options are much better than the canned fruit we used to get. My friends and I still wish there were more variety in the main dishes, though. Some days the choices feel a bit repetitive. Overall, I think it is a positive change, and I have noticed I feel more energetic in the afternoon compared to last year."

Text C — Article: Nutrition and Learning

"Research has consistently shown that proper nutrition plays a vital role in students' ability to learn and concentrate. Diets rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains provide the essential nutrients that support brain function and sustained energy levels. On the other hand, meals high in processed sugars and unhealthy fats can lead to energy crashes and difficulty focusing. Schools that have improved their meal programs often report better classroom behavior and higher test scores over time."
